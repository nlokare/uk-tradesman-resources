### How to Calculate Your Sustainable Hourly Rate

Your rate must cover more than your desired take-home pay. Use this formula:

```
Required annual revenue
= (Desired net salary ÷ (1 - income tax rate))
+ National Insurance contributions
+ Public liability insurance (~£400–£800/yr)
+ Van, fuel, and vehicle costs (~£3,000–£8,000/yr)
+ Tools and equipment (~£500–£2,000/yr)
+ Training and certification (~£300–£1,000/yr)
+ Unpaid admin, quoting, and travel time (allow 20–30%)

Billable hours per year
= Working weeks (46 after holiday) × billable hours per week (typically 25–32)

Minimum hourly rate = Required annual revenue ÷ Billable hours
```

**Example:** A self-employed electrician targeting £40,000 net income needs approximately £85,000–£95,000 in gross revenue to cover taxes, NI, and overheads. At 1,200 billable hours per year, that requires a minimum rate of £71–£79/hour.

## Quoting & Invoicing

### What a Professional Quote Must Include

Under UK consumer contract regulations, a written quote should include:

- [ ] Your full name or business name and address
- [ ] Your contact details (phone, email)
- [ ] The customer's name and address
- [ ] A clear description of the work to be carried out
- [ ] Start date and estimated duration
- [ ] Itemised costs: labour and materials listed separately
- [ ] VAT status — if VAT registered, show VAT number and amount
- [ ] Payment terms (e.g. 50% deposit, balance on completion)
- [ ] Quote validity period (typically 30 days)
- [ ] Any assumptions or exclusions

### Quote vs Estimate: Legal Distinction

**Quote:** A fixed price for a defined scope. If the customer accepts, you are legally bound to that price unless the scope changes.

**Estimate:** An approximate figure. You can charge more or less than stated, but you must inform the customer if costs will materially exceed the estimate before proceeding.

**Best practice:** Always use the word "Quote" on fixed-price work. Reserve "Estimate" only when the scope genuinely cannot be determined upfront (e.g. drain investigations, damp diagnosis).

## Legal & Compliance

### Contracts

- All contracts over £1 for services must comply with the **Consumer Rights Act 2015** when the customer is a consumer (not a business).
- For work over £10,000 to a consumer, consider using a **JCT Minor Works Contract** or similar standard form.
- Always get a signed acceptance of your quote before starting work. Email acceptance is legally binding.

### Dispute Resolution

- Membership of a trade association (NICEIC, Gas Safe, NAPIT, FMB) provides access to dispute resolution schemes.
- For debts under £10,000, use the **Money Claims Online** (MCOL) service.
- Citizens Advice Bureau provides free guidance on trade contract disputes.

### Right to Cancel

Under the **Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013**, consumers who sign up for services at their home (or off-premises) have a 14-day cooling-off period. This applies to quoting visits where the customer signs on the spot. Best practice: do not start work within 14 days unless the customer signs a waiver.

## Tax & VAT

### Income Tax and National Insurance (2025/26)

Self-employed tradespeople pay:

- **Class 2 NI:** £3.45 per week (flat rate)
- **Class 4 NI:** 9% on profits £12,570–£50,270; 2% above £50,270
- **Income Tax:** 0% up to £12,570 / 20% on £12,571–£50,270 / 40% above £50,270

**Self Assessment deadlines:**
- 31 January: online return and payment for previous tax year
- 31 July: payment on account for current year

### VAT

- **Mandatory VAT registration threshold:** £90,000 turnover in any rolling 12-month period (2025/26)
- **Standard rate:** 20%
- **Reduced rate:** 5% applies to certain energy-saving materials and home adaptations
- **Zero rate:** New residential builds (subject to conditions)

**VAT reverse charge** applies to most B2B construction services where the customer is VAT registered. This means the customer accounts for the VAT, not the supplier. Check HMRC guidance for the full list of affected services.

### Making Tax Digital (MTD)

MTD for Income Tax Self Assessment (ITSA) begins for sole traders and landlords earning over £50,000 from April 2026, and over £30,000 from April 2027. This requires quarterly digital submissions to HMRC through compatible accounting software.

## Marketing Your Trade Business

### Getting Found Online

1. **Google Business Profile** — Free and the most important local SEO asset for tradespeople. Keep it updated with photos, services, and prompt responses to reviews.
2. **Checkatrade / Rated People / MyBuilder** — Lead generation platforms. Useful early on but high ongoing cost. Aim to replace with organic enquiries over time.
3. **A professional website** — Even a simple site with your services, area, and contact form adds credibility and captures organic search traffic.
4. **Google Reviews** — Ask every satisfied customer for a review immediately after job completion. A consistent flow of 5-star reviews outperforms paid ads for local search.

### Social Media for Tradespeople

- **Before/after photos** on Instagram and Facebook are the highest-performing content for trades.
- Document unusual jobs, tricky installs, and time-lapse work videos.
- Consistency matters more than production quality — phone camera + good lighting is enough.
